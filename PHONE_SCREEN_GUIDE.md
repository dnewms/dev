# Phone Screen Guide
## Applications Programmer Associate - Robotics
## University of Michigan

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes
**Format:** Initial screening call
**Interviewer Prep Time:** 2-3 minutes to review candidate's resume/highlights

---

## INTERVIEW STRUCTURE

### 1. Introduction (2 minutes)
- Introduce yourself and your role
- Brief overview of the position and Robotics department
- Confirm candidate's availability and interest
- Set expectations for the call

**Script:**
> "Thank you for your interest in the Applications Programmer Associate position with Michigan Robotics. This will be a brief 15-20 minute conversation to learn more about your background and discuss the role. Do you have time to chat now?"

---

### 2. Candidate Background (5-7 minutes)

#### A. Current Situation
- "Tell me about your current role and what you're working on."
- "What's prompting you to explore this opportunity at University of Michigan?"

**Listen for:**
- Genuine interest in higher education/research environment
- Understanding of university culture (especially from internal/higher ed candidates)
- Career trajectory alignment

#### B. Technical Experience Quick Check
Select 2-3 questions based on candidate's background:

**Full-Stack Development:**
- "I see you have experience with [React/Angular/Vue]. Can you briefly describe a recent project where you built both frontend and backend components?"
- "What's your typical development workflow when starting a new feature?"

**Cloud/DevOps:**
- "Tell me about your experience with cloud platforms like AWS or Azure. What services have you used?"
- "How do you approach deployment and CI/CD in your current/recent projects?"

**Problem-Solving:**
- "Describe a challenging technical problem you recently solved. What was your approach?"

**Listen for:**
- Clear communication of technical concepts
- Practical hands-on experience (not just theoretical knowledge)
- Problem-solving methodology

#### C. Collaboration & Communication
- "This role involves working with faculty, researchers, and students. Can you share an example of working with non-technical stakeholders?"
- "How do you typically handle competing priorities or urgent requests?"

**Listen for:**
- Communication skills with diverse audiences
- Flexibility and adaptability
- Service-oriented mindset (critical for higher ed)

---

### 3. Role Expectations & Fit (3-5 minutes)

#### Key Responsibilities Overview:
> "This position supports the Michigan Robotics department by developing and maintaining web applications, data systems, and research tools. You'd be working with cutting-edge robotics research while building production systems that support faculty, students, and research projects."

**Key Points to Cover:**
- Mix of maintenance and new development
- Supporting research activities and educational programs
- Occasional urgent requests and diverse project types
- Collaborative environment with researchers and students

#### Questions to Ask:
- "Does this mix of research support and application development appeal to you?"
- "What interests you most about working in a robotics/research environment?"
- **For higher ed candidates:** "What do you enjoy about working in academia/higher education?"
- **For non-higher ed candidates:** "What attracts you to moving into higher education?"

**Listen for:**
- Enthusiasm for mission-driven work
- Interest in learning and diverse challenges
- Patience for academic environment pace/processes
- Curiosity about robotics (don't need deep robotics knowledge)

---

### 4. Logistics & Next Steps (2-3 minutes)

#### Confirm Key Details:
- **Availability:** "What's your timeline for a potential start date?"
- **Location:** "This role is [hybrid/on-campus]. Are you comfortable with that arrangement?"
- **Work Authorization:** "Are you authorized to work in the US?" *(if applicable)*
- **Salary Expectations:** "Do you have salary expectations for this role?" *(optional for phone screen)*

#### Assess Red Flags (if applicable):
- **For candidates with gaps:** "I noticed a gap in [timeframe]. Can you tell me what you were focused on during that time?"
- **For internal candidates on RIF:** *(Julie Keros)* "I understand you're currently on RIF status. Can you help me understand your situation and availability?"
- **For students:** *(Stefan Mitroi, Jason Cava)* "I see you're graduating in 2026. Are you looking for full-time or part-time work?"

#### Closing:
- Answer any questions the candidate has
- Explain next steps in the process
- Timeline for follow-up

**Script:**
> "Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. We're reviewing candidates this week and will be scheduling on-site/technical interviews with selected candidates. You should hear back from us by [date]. Do you have any questions for me?"

## RED FLAGS TO WATCH FOR

During the call, be alert for:

1. **Communication Issues:**
   - Difficulty explaining technical concepts clearly
   - Unable to provide concrete examples
   - Overly verbose or unfocused responses

2. **Motivation Concerns:**
   - Primarily interested in visa sponsorship only
   - Unclear why they're interested in higher education
   - Just looking for "any job"
   - Unrealistic salary expectations for the role level

3. **Availability Issues:**
   - Still in school with unclear graduation date
   - Long notice period (>1 month) without justification
   - Part-time only when full-time is required

4. **Experience Misrepresentation:**
   - Can't speak to technologies listed on resume
   - Vague about actual responsibilities
   - Takes credit for team work without acknowledging team

5. **Culture Fit Concerns:**
   - Only wants prestigious research, not willing to do maintenance
   - Impatient with process/bureaucracy (common in academia)
   - Poor attitude toward supporting others' work

## TIPS FOR EFFECTIVE PHONE SCREENS

### DO:
✅ Review candidate's resume 2-3 minutes before the call
✅ Be warm and welcoming - you're representing UMich
✅ Listen more than you talk (70/30 rule)
✅ Take notes during the call for later reference
✅ Give candidates a chance to ask questions
✅ Be transparent about timeline and next steps
✅ Sell the opportunity - top candidates have options

### DON'T:
❌ Ask overly technical questions (save for technical interview)
❌ Make promises about timeline or decisions
❌ Spend too much time on any one topic
❌ Forget to assess communication skills (critical for this role)
❌ Skip the logistics questions
❌ Rush - this is their first impression of UMich
